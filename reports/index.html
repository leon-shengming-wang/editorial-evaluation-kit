editorial / ed-001

Write a factual headline of no more than 12 words. Preserve the distinction between a proposal and an approved decision.

Synthetic exercise · AI-assisted example

Source of truth

Fictional council briefing: On Tuesday, the Riverton council proposed extending library hours to 9 p.m. A final vote is scheduled for next month. No extension has been approved.

Response A

Riverton council approves library hours extension until 9 p.m.

Weighted score: 9/20

accuracy 1 · instructions 2 · clarity 5

Response B

Riverton council proposes later library hours; vote due next month

Weighted score: 20/20

accuracy 5 · instructions 5 · clarity 5

Decision: B

B preserves the proposal status and the pending vote in 11 whitespace-delimited words. A incorrectly converts a proposal into approval. A is concise and readable, but those strengths cannot repair its central factual error.

  • A approves
    The source explicitly says no extension has been approved.
  • B proposes later library hours
    This accurately describes the council action without asserting adoption.

Limit: No external fact checking is needed for this fictional, closed-source exercise.

fact-check / fc-001

Check the claim that attendance doubled. State the percentage increase using the supplied figures.

Synthetic exercise · AI-assisted example

Source of truth

Fictional attendance table: Last year, 240 visitors attended. This year, 360 visitors attended. Both counts cover the same event and duration.

Response A

Attendance doubled, increasing by 100% from 240 to 360.

Weighted score: 9/20

accuracy 1 · instructions 2 · clarity 5

Response B

Attendance increased by 50%, not 100%: (360 - 240) / 240 = 0.5.

Weighted score: 20/20

accuracy 5 · instructions 5 · clarity 5

Decision: B

The absolute increase is 120, and the baseline is 240, producing 50%. B explicitly checks the doubled claim and shows the calculation. A repeats the false claim despite reproducing the correct raw counts.

  • A increasing by 100%
    120 divided by the 240 baseline is 50%, not 100%.
  • B (360 - 240) / 240 = 0.5
    Uses the correct difference and baseline.

Limit: The comparison assumes the supplied attendance counts are valid; the prompt provides no independent audit of them.

editorial / ed-004

Write one sentence of no more than 25 whitespace-delimited words for a general audience; include the survey result, its limited sample, and the uncertainty about all visitors.

Synthetic exercise · AI-assisted example

Source of truth

Fictional museum survey: 72 of 100 visitors who voluntarily answered an online questionnaire preferred longer evening hours. Participation was optional and the sample was not randomly selected. The museum cannot establish the preference of all visitors from these responses.

Response A

In a voluntary online survey, 72 of 100 museum visitors preferred longer evening hours.

Weighted score: 18/20

accuracy 5 · instructions 3 · clarity 5

Response B

Among 100 visitors who voluntarily answered an online questionnaire, 72 preferred longer evening hours; the nonrandom sample does not establish whether all museum visitors share that preference.

Weighted score: 19/20

accuracy 5 · instructions 4 · clarity 5

Decision: B

A accurately limits its numeric claim to survey participants and is concise; it does not falsely claim population representativeness. However, it omits the explicitly requested uncertainty about all visitors. B states the result and limitation clearly, but its 27 whitespace-delimited words exceed the 25-word cap. B narrowly wins 19 to 18 under this rubric: a length breach is less serious here than omitting a requested interpretive safeguard. Neither response fully meets the brief; B should be shortened before publication. An evaluator who treats the word cap as a strict acceptance gate could reasonably reject both rather than publish B.

  • A In a voluntary online survey
    Accurately scopes the numeric claim but does not explicitly explain why it cannot establish the preference of all visitors.
  • B the nonrandom sample does not establish whether all museum visitors share that preference
    States the required uncertainty without claiming that other visitors disagree.

Limit: The underlying population preference is unknown, but there is enough evidence to judge whether each summary preserves that uncertainty. This is therefore a preference decision, not an abstention. The severity assigned to the word-limit breach is an authored calibration choice, not an independently validated rating.

design / ds-004

Compare these written exhibition-panel proposals, not rendered artwork. The panel must include a title, opening date, and archive credit. It should explain the exhibition plainly to first-time visitors. Judge fidelity to the supplied brief separately from compliance and clarity.

Synthetic exercise · AI-assisted example

Source of truth

Fictional exhibition brief: Title: Neighborhood Voices. Opening date: June 8. Required credit: Riverton Community Archive. The exhibition presents residents' recorded memories of local streets. The supplied brief does not specify typeface, font size, colors, or physical dimensions.

Response A

Title: Neighborhood Voices. Opens June 8. Credit: Riverton Community Archive. Introductory text: This exhibition foregrounds the polyvocal mnemonic textures of quotidian urban spatiality through residents' recorded memories of local streets.

Weighted score: 18/20

accuracy 5 · instructions 5 · clarity 3

Response B

Title: Neighborhood Voices. Introductory text: Hear residents share their memories of local streets. The opening date and archive credit will be omitted from this panel to keep the message simple.

Weighted score: 18/20

accuracy 5 · instructions 3 · clarity 5

Decision: tie

Both proposals faithfully describe their own planned content and make no contradictory factual claim about the exhibition. A includes all mandatory factual information but uses unnecessary jargon in its introduction, weakening accessibility for first-time visitors. In this exercise that defect is scored under clarity rather than counted twice. B explains the exhibition plainly but explicitly omits two required elements. Each totals 18, so the deterministic rule yields a tie despite materially different defects. This is not a claim of equivalent publishability: a production workflow with mandatory-credit gates should reject B. The appropriate revision combines A's date and credit with B's plain-language introduction.

  • A polyvocal mnemonic textures of quotidian urban spatiality
    Abstract jargon makes a simple exhibition description harder to understand; this is a text clarity issue, not evidence about unseen visual layout.
  • A Opens June 8. Credit: Riverton Community Archive.
    Includes both required details accurately.
  • B The opening date and archive credit will be omitted
    Truthfully states a planned omission that violates the brief; score it under compliance rather than treating the proposal itself as a false factual claim.
  • B Hear residents share their memories of local streets.
    Clearly conveys the exhibition's content without specialist terminology.

Limit: No rendered panels, dimensions, or accessibility measurements are provided. Typography, visual hierarchy, contrast, and actual readability cannot be assessed. The tie exposes a limitation of additive scoring; it does not certify either proposal as ready for publication. Ratings are authored synthetic judgments, not independent reviewer agreement.

Scores help make judgments consistent; they do not replace checking the source. The validator checks structure and internal consistency, not whether a factual claim is true.
